More tourists, more rental profit? I tested that assumption for Bergamo.

Official city guest nights rose 52.7% between 2019 and 2024. But more visitors do not automatically make short-term renting the better business choice.

I reconciled 21 historical listing records with 20 preliminary leads and 55 additional advertisements. After checking contract terms, separating charges and reviewing property identity, the strict central furnished one-bedroom sample contained 14 properties.

Their base asking rent averaged € 894.29 per month, with a median of € 850. The 20-property target was not reached, and the criteria stayed unchanged.

Using the unrounded mean and the same expense assumptions, the Python model gives € 6,677.83 annual net cash for long-term renting versus € 6,544.50 short-term: a gap of only € 133.33. These are conditional model outcomes, not observed profits.

One research challenge mattered: a € 900 mid-term package could not be treated as € 900 conventional base rent. Another property appeared across platforms with conflicting prices, so it was excluded.

The historical € 900 scenario remains preserved. Sensitivity analysis shows why paid months, operating costs and conversion spending can matter more than this small cash difference.
